Put the notch
to work.

That black strip at the top of your MacBook. Noki opens it up and keeps what's playing, what's on today, translation, notes and a place to park files all in one spot.

Download Free / 0.1.0

Once it downloads, move Noki into your Applications folder and open it.

What it does

Music

What's playing shows up either side of the notch, with the artwork and a waveform. Press the waveform to pause and start again. Works with Music, Spotify and anything playing in a browser.

Calendar

This week's dates and what's on that day. Swipe two fingers to move up to two weeks either way, and scroll a busy day.

Translation

Type and it translates as you go. Either side takes typing, and one tap swaps them. macOS does the translating on the device, so nothing you type leaves your Mac.

Notes

Write down whatever just came to mind. It saves as you type, and the notch stays open while you write.

Shortcuts

Drop in the apps and files you reach for often and they become tiles. Anything in the Shortcuts app can go there too.

File tray

Drag a file onto the notch and it waits there. Send it on with AirDrop, or drag it wherever it needs to go.

Clear the desktop in one press

One press on the menu bar icon and the clutter on your desktop disappears. Handy before you share your screen or hand your Mac to someone. Press again and it's back. Nothing is deleted.

The first time you open it

  1. Put it in Applications

    Move the downloaded Noki into your Applications folder before you open it.

  2. Grant two permissions

    Accessibility (to show volume and brightness, and to use the media keys) and Calendar (to show what's on). Both have a Grant button in the settings window. Skip the ones for features you won't use.

  3. Point at the notch

    It can open on hover or on a click — your choice. Right-click the notch to reach the settings.
